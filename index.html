Model & units

Tidal acceleration. Across a star of radius r at distance R the differential pull is a_tid ≈ 2GM·r/R³. It stretches the star radially and squeezes it by GM·r/R³ transversely. Disruption starts near r_t ≈ R★(M_BH/M★)^1/3, where τ = a_tid/a_self ≈ (r_t/R)³ reaches 1.

Dynamics. Gas parcels feel the black hole's gravity (Paczyński–Wiita potential Φ = −GM/(R−r_s), which mimics the innermost stable orbit at 3r_s) and, while still bound to the star, a linear self-gravity/pressure restoring force −ω₀²Δx, with ω₀² = 2GM/r_t³. Freed debris follows ballistic orbits (symplectic Euler, adaptive sub-steps). Its specific energy ε = v²/2 + Φ decides whether it is bound (ε<0) or unbound (ε>0). Returning streams lose energy near pericenter, which stands in for stream–stream shocks, then join a viscous disk and drain inward.

Scale. Code units with G = 1. One scene unit is 6.25 R☉ ≈ 0.029 AU and one code time unit is ≈ 17.6 min. The horizon-to-tidal-radius ratio is physical. The star is drawn ×15 larger than true scale so its structure is visible. That also widens the debris energy spread, so fallback happens much sooner than in nature (the real tfb appears above). Disk rotation inside 10 rs is visually slowed.

Visualization uses a simplified Newtonian/relativistic-inspired model for educational purposes; it is not a full relativistic hydrodynamic simulation.
